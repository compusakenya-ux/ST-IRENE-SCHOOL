Owned and operated by the Sisters of St. Joseph — and it shows in every corridor.

In 1974, a small band of Sisters of St. Joseph — a Catholic congregation of religious women — crossed from Mombasa Island with two blackboards, a bell, and a conviction that the children of Mikindani deserved schooling of both excellence and tenderness. They began with nineteen pupils under a mango tree.

Fifty-two years later, that mango shade has grown into a full K–12 campus. The Sisters still own and operate the school: they sit on the Board of Governors, teach, serve as chaplain and counsellors, and personally greet the buses each morning. Every policy here passes a simple test the foundresses set — does this form the whole child?

"We do not only prepare children for examinations. We prepare them for life — with Christ in the heart and competence in the hands." — Rev. Sr. Mary-Cecilia, OSJ · School Superior

02 · Maadili — The Four Virtues

What we build into every child

II

The virtues are not posters on our walls — they are marked, mentored and celebrated every single term.

I.

Honesty

Uaminifu

Truth spoken kindly, even when it costs. Our learners keep honour-code pledges during assessments and lead their own parent conferences from Grade 4 upward.

"Let your word be enough."— MATTHEW 5:37
II.

Dedication

Jitihada

Finishing what is begun. Every learner keeps a long-term project — a garden plot, a codebase, a choir part — carried across years, not weeks.

"Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart."— COLOSSIANS 3:23
III.

Servant Leadership

Uongozi wa Huduma

The greatest among us serves. Prefects are appointed by lot after a term of service; Grade 11s mentor KG reading circles every Friday.

"Whoever wishes to be great must become a servant."— MARK 10:43
IV.

Hard Work

Bidii

Hands busy, minds awake. Our school farm in Mikindani feeds the kitchen, and every class keeps its own corner of the shamba — rain or shine.

"Do you see one skilled in their work? They will serve before kings."— PROVERBS 22:29

Where four-year-olds meet their first computer and their first catechism.

Kindergarten at St. Irene is bilingual from day one — Kiswahili and English woven through songs, sand tables and story circles. PP2 learners take weekly "first touch" sessions in the ICT Suite, learning mouse, keys and logic games before they can tie their shoes.

Reading, counting, praying — the three anchors of the early years.

Foundational literacy and numeracy are taught in small guided groups, with a Sister or teacher mentoring each reading circle. Learners begin formal Computer Studies exposure and join the school shamba rota, planting their first crops.

04 · Teknolojia — Screens with a Soul

Technology taught from the age of four.

Two dedicated computer suites, a robotics bench and a rule we have kept since 1998: every learner touches a keyboard every week, from PP2 to Grade 12. Technology here is a tool for service — not a toy — and digital citizenship is taught as seriously as the catechism.

09 · Uandikishaji — Admissions

Four gentle steps to Karibu.

  1. Visit & observe

    Tours run Tuesdays and Thursdays at 10:00. See the suites, the shamba and the chapel; bring your questions and your child's laughter.

  2. Interview & assessment

    A friendly, age-appropriate meeting with teachers. KG applicants are assessed through play; transfer learners bring their CBE records.

  3. Offer & documents

    Birth certificate, photos, baptism certificate (where available), previous report forms — then a signed offer and fee schedule.

  4. Karibu nyumbani

    Orientation day: house colours assigned, bus route confirmed, uniform fitted. The bell does the rest.

10 · Maswali — Asked of the Office

Questions parents actually ask

X

Yes — children of all faiths are warmly admitted. Christian Religious Education follows the CBE syllabus, and all learners join assemblies and the school's moral programme with respect. No child is ever pressured toward baptism or membership.

The main intake is in January for KG1 and Grade 1. Mid-term and mid-year transfers are accepted subject to space and transfer documentation from the previous CBE school. The 2027 intake interviews run 23–27 November 2026.

CBE — Curriculum-Based Education — is Kenya's competency-based approach: learning is organised around what a child can demonstrably do, assessed continuously through school-based assessment moderated by KNEC, from Pre-Primary through the Grade 6 transition to Grade 12 and KCSE. St. Irene implements the full national pathway.

Boarding is term-based, from Grade 4 upward, with 60 beds in separate girls' and boys' wings under a resident matron and a patron Sister. Supervised holiday stay is available on request for families travelling.

Transport is an extra cost, billed per term according to route (KSh 8,500–21,000). Every bus carries a chaperone — a teacher or Sister — and seats are allocated at enrolment. Siblings riding the same route receive a 10% discount.

All learners receive cooked breakfast (boarders and early arrivals) and a hot lunch, much of it from our own Mikindani shamba. Boarders take full board. We accommodate noted allergies and dietary observances.
